Nessa is the kind of person who makes an ordinary Tuesday feel like it's worth photographing. This issue is a small, completely biased attempt to put that on record — the food, the films, the way she sees things — from someone who's been too busy to say it out loud, and decided to build it instead.

Feature 01

Appetite

She will try anything once — and she's almost always right about it.

Hand her a menu in a language neither of us reads and she orders the strangest thing on it, on purpose. That's the bit I love. Most people order safety. Nessa orders curiosity and then makes you taste it.

Sweet markets, street food, the place with no sign and a queue out the door — wherever the good stuff is, she's already three steps ahead, phone out, deciding what we're getting.

Verdict: a coconut by the water beats a reservation she had to plan for. She'd rather find it than book it.

Feature 02

Screen Time

She talks about movies like they matter. Turns out they do.

Somewhere between "you have to watch this" and "no, tonight," Nessa got me to start Attack on Titan — and she was right, which she'll never let me forget. So here's it in writing: you were right.

In return I sent her toward Game of Thrones, which she still owes me. The deal stands. The deal is, frankly, the point — it means there's always a next time.

She → me Attack on Titan Watched. Emotionally compromised. Worth it.
⇄the trade
Me → her Game of Thrones Outstanding. Payment due in episodes.
Feature 03

Through Her Lens

She notices the things the rest of us walk straight past.

A rooftop at the exact minute the sky goes from blue to gold. A reflection. A sign that says THEATRE behind her like it was placed there on purpose. Then she photographs it — and suddenly I see it too.

That's the quiet thing about being around someone with an eye: the world gets more interesting just by standing next to them. I've started noticing the light because she does.

Editor's Letter

A note I kept
meaning to send

Nessa — I've been bad at being around lately. Not bad at thinking about you; I've got that part down. "Busy" is just the word I keep using so I don't have to admit I should've made more time.

So I made this instead. Consider it the long version of a text I kept meaning to send.

You're the most interesting person I'm not doing enough about. You'll try any food once. You talk about movies like they matter — and somehow got me into Attack on Titan (you were right, again). You still owe me Game of Thrones. You see things other people miss, and then you photograph them, and suddenly I see them too.

And you're direct, which I've decided is my favourite thing about you. So I'll match it: I think about you. A lot. That's the whole headline. No pressure attached — I just didn't want another busy week to pass without you knowing.

— Daniel
